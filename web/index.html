Tiderip

Tiderip shows you where and when wind will fight the tidal current in the Salish Sea — the conditions that turn a pleasant run into short, steep chop. It combines UBC's SalishSeaCast current forecast with Environment Canada's high-resolution wind forecast, then paints the water with a simple question in mind: would a small boat be comfortable here, at this hour? It covers the Gulf Islands, Saanich Inlet and Sidney, the San Juans, and the waters between Nanaimo, Bellingham, and Race Rocks, roughly two days out. Load it once while you have signal and everything keeps working offline. It's a planning aid built on model forecasts — not a navigation chart, and no substitute for official current tables, your charts, or your own eyes.

Set it to your boat

Open the settings gear (top right). Three thresholds — minimum wind, minimum current, and your comfort-max wind — define what counts as trouble on the map, and they're remembered between visits. The defaults suit a very small power boat.

Read the map

Colors mark trouble: yellow-to-red where wind opposes current strongly enough to cross your thresholds (deeper red = worse), cobalt blue where wind alone exceeds your comfort max, faint grey-teal where wind opposes current but too weakly to care. Clear water means no flags — check the wind arrows before calling it calm. Arrows show wind (dark) or current (teal) or both; longer arrow = stronger. Switch them in settings.

Ask about anywhere

Tap any point of water for the numbers behind the colors: wind, current, and opposition score. The rip bar — the time strip at the bottom — follows your tap, and the yellow star on the map marks the point it's showing. Tap the ✕ next to the bar to return to the default (Boundary Pass). Magenta diamonds mark common passes and crossings — tap one, or pick from the list in settings.

Scrub through time

The rip bar covers the whole forecast, hour by hour, for the starred location. Bar height and color show how rough the wind-against-current gets; the teal curve is current speed, so its valleys (circled) are slack and its peaks are max ebb or flood. Drag across the bar to move the map through time, or press play.

Plan a trip

In settings → Routes, set your cruise speed and travel window, then tap waypoints on the map and save (speed and window are captured when you save). With a route selected, each rip-bar hour becomes a possible departure time: green = clear run, warm = wind-against- current somewhere en route, blue = wind over your comfort, grey = outside your window or the forecast. Scrub to a departure hour and the route repaints with what you'd meet along the way.

Trust, but verify

Magenta rings are real weather stations showing the latest measured wind next to what the model claimed. The header shows the forecast's age; a warning banner appears if it's stale. Model currents in narrow passes are often right on timing and wrong on strength — keep the official tables for pass transits.
